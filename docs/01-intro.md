## Introduction

#### Let’s talk about the Paza Playbook and why it matters

Imagine you’re building a speech model from scratch — whether it’s for recognising speech through *Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR)* or generating audio with *Text to Speech (TTS)*. The Paza Playbook is your guide, packed with practical tips and best practices, especially for languages where data is hard to come by. 

Why focus on low-resource languages? Well, these are languages that might be spoken by millions, or just a small community, but they all share one thing: there’s very little publicly available data for them online. That means most AI models don’t “see” these languages during training, which leads to bias and makes life harder for machine learning experts trying to support them.

The playbook isn’t just for African languages—it’s a toolkit that can be adapted to any language, as long as you have some data to work with. But its heart is in helping those working with low-resource and multilingual environments, making sure everyone has a fair shot at building robust speech models.

####  Who is this for? 
It’s designed to help researchers, engineers, and anyone interested in speech technology tackle real-world challenges like limited data, diverse languages, tricky acoustics, and tight computational budgets.


The aim of this playbook is to equip NLP/ML researchers, engineers, and practitioners with the tools and insights necessary for successful model training, deployment, and evaluation. The guidelines in this playbook can be used to extend speech models to any language with available data, but the focus is low-resource languages.

#### Definition
> Low Resource Languages are languages either spoken by millions of people or a handful of people that have minimal publicly available datasets on the web. This means that Generative AI models lack language representation for it during training, creating a bias, inequality, and a bigger challenge for ML experts working on these languages.
